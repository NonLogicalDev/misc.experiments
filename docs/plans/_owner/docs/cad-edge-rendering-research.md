# CAD edge rendering research

**Working note, 2026-11-11.** The goal is to show and select B-rep edges on shaded faces, including the visible portions of partly occluded edges. The options below are candidates for comparison; none has been verified as the best approach for SolidRecipe.

## What must be rendered

A CAD edge is a topological entity that may be curved and belongs to one or more faces. It is not generally the same thing as an edge of a display triangle. A generic triangle-wireframe shader would expose triangulation diagonals and would not automatically supply the B-rep index needed for selection. Face silhouettes are view-dependent and are not necessarily B-rep edges. [PyVista's CAD visualization guide](https://cad.pyvista.org/user_guide/cad_visualization.html) describes the common arrangement of smooth shaded faces and tessellated B-rep feature curves.

SolidRecipe's tessellator already provides face-associated triangles and edge polylines from the same B-rep record. Preserve that association and snapshot-scoped selection when comparing rendering approaches. Do not treat display polylines or sampled normals as the authority for exact geometry or persistent identity.

## Candidate approaches

| Approach | Potential benefit | Cost or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Current B-rep lines over shaded faces** | Draw the actual topology edges with screen-space widths and pickable indices. Depth testing hides lines behind faces. | Coplanar lines and faces can produce differing depth values; insufficient bias produces gaps and excessive bias reveals hidden lines. Fat line quads can be partially occluded by a neighboring surface. Ordering must also ensure normal outlines do not cover selected ones. Bias is a compromise that requires testing, not a proof of correct visibility. |
| **Single-pass lines in face shading** | Draws edges as part of the same face fragments, avoiding separate coplanar face/line depth tests for those boundaries. The [2008 Bærentzen et al. paper](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/3735323/wire-sccg.pdf) studies this and an ID-buffer method with filtered lines. | A generic barycentric triangle wireframe shows every triangle edge. To show only B-rep boundaries, each triangle side needs reliable boundary and selection metadata, including the mapping of split polylines to mesh sides. Edge silhouettes, partly hidden lines, seams and exact picking still need an explicit design. The 2008 paper is relevant prior art, not evidence that its method is today's overall state of the art. |
| **ID-buffer or ownership-aware visibility** | A separate face identity buffer can help determine whether a line fragment belongs to the face visible at a pixel. The 2008 paper studies an ID-buffer technique for general polygons. | Requires extra passes, memory, anti-aliasing and an explicit policy for edges shared by faces, coincident geometry and identity at silhouettes. Evaluate WebGL support and measured cost before adoption; the paper does not establish that a particular implementation solves our cases. |
| **Analytic or direct B-rep rendering** | Evaluating curves and surfaces directly could reduce faceting and avoid making every visible contour a tessellated line. [NVIDIA's GPU vector-art chapter](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iv-image-effects/chapter-25-rendering-vector-art-gpu) illustrates direct evaluation of some spline curves. | General trimmed B-rep surfaces, intersections, visibility and picking need substantial specialized algorithms. The cited vector-art work is not a complete CAD renderer. A newer [parameter-preserving BIM rendering paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cav.70159) explores direct GPU ray tracing, but its supported geometry, hardware and performance claims must be evaluated for SolidRecipe rather than assumed transferable. |

The [Three.js LineMaterial documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LineMaterial.html) specifies screen-space width in CSS pixels, viewport-resolution handling and alpha-to-coverage when multisampling is available. The [OpenGL polygon-offset FAQ](https://www.opengl.org/archives/resources/faq/technical/polygonoffset.htm) explains why coincident lines and filled polygons can disagree in depth and why offset may cause bleed-through. The [Three.js Material documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Material.html) identifies polygon offset as a tool for highlighted edges and hidden-line images; it does not guarantee an artifact-free result.

## Candidate triangle-side mapping experiment

The tessellator already constructs useful integer metadata before triangulation. For each face boundary loop, `Boundary.segments` associates a loop segment with a B-rep record edge index and its sampled segment ordinal; it accounts for reversed edge uses and loop rotation. That metadata is currently used for boundary untangling, then discarded when the planner passes only loop coordinates into face planning. It does not itself include a distinct edge-use ID. [Boundary construction and planning](../../../../vendor/solidcraft/solidcore/crates/solidcore-tess/src/mesher/mod.rs).

As a limited experiment, retain an explicit loop/use identifier and carry the segment labels through polygon construction. For faces without seam cutting, pole duplication, duplicate UV nodes or crossings, associate each local polygon node-index pair with its labeled boundary segment. Attach the label to the corresponding side of each output triangle and propagate it alongside the triangle and face index. Use node indices rather than matching floating-point positions. Reorder side labels when face winding changes; report incomplete or ambiguous mapping and retain B-rep polylines as the fallback. The current [face plan and evaluated mesh](../../../../vendor/solidcraft/solidcore/crates/solidcore-tess/src/plan.rs) do not provide this triangle-side mapping.

This limited path is not a general solution. [Constrained triangulation](../../../../vendor/solidcraft/solidcore/crates/solidcore-tess/src/mesher/cdt.rs) can merge duplicate UV handles; slit boundaries can use a side twice, so a side may have multiple uses. [Periodic seam cutting](../../../../vendor/solidcraft/solidcore/crates/solidcore-tess/src/mesher/seam.rs) copies and clips boundary runs and introduces synthetic sides, while pole processing can duplicate and renumber vertices. Preserving labels through those operations needs explicit provenance and ambiguity rules. Derive segment ordinals from the final prepared boundary because untangling can resample edges.

Validate a prototype against the box, adapter, cylindrical and filleted faces, then exercise reversed winding, holes, slits, coincident edges, periodic seams, poles and lenient triangulation. Check every labeled side against its actual face/edge use, detect missing or multiply claimed boundary segments, and confirm that internal triangle sides are not labeled as CAD edges. These are candidate future tests; the mapping has not been implemented or verified.

## Controlled comparison

Use the same topology, camera paths, materials and selected entities for each candidate. Capture screenshots or short recordings and record renderer settings and device pixel ratio. Compare against actual B-rep entity inspection, not only visual similarity. At minimum cover:

1. **Visible and hidden edges:** a front edge, a fully hidden rear edge, and an edge that passes behind another face partway along its length. Check that visible portions do not break up and hidden portions do not leak through.
2. **Silhouettes and steep angles:** rotate a box, cylinder and adapter; inspect near-tangent views, edge-on faces and depth discontinuities. Distinguish a view-dependent silhouette from a selectable B-rep edge.
3. **Selection:** select edges and faces, then hover another edge, overlap selected and unselected lines, and confirm highlight color and the picked entity remain correct. Test the identity-buffer or boundary-metadata mapping against the same snapshot indices.
4. **Seams and coincident geometry:** include a cylindrical seam, tangent continuation, adjacent faces, and coincident or near-coincident boundaries. Specify which lines should be shown and whether each is selectable; do not infer distinct identities from overlapping pixels.
5. **Resolution and zoom:** compare DPR 1 and 2, multisampling on and unavailable, window resizing, close and distant zoom, perspective and any supported orthographic camera. Report line width in CSS pixels and check it remains stable.
6. **Performance:** measure initial preparation, rebuild payload, per-frame CPU/GPU time and memory on the adapter and a larger model. Include the cost of selection updates, extra buffers or shader metadata; do not claim an improvement without measurement.

Use the current depth-tested B-rep line rendering as a baseline. Change one variable at a time, such as draw order, depth bias or antialiasing, and retain the baseline captures. If no calibrated bias satisfies both visibility and occlusion cases, prototype a boundary-aware or ownership-aware alternative rather than adding increasingly large offsets.

## Controlled diagnostics

The following observations are from experimental diagnostics, not an accepted rendering design. The diagnostic code was removed after the checks; no production rendering change was adopted from these experiments.

- **Line width and depth bias:** Disabling depth testing on a 3 CSS-pixel Line2 restored its full width, but the line crossed an occluding surface. An experimental line polygon offset of factor `-2` and units `-8` improved visible width; close surfaces still showed an apparent line over a gray occluder. That observation cannot be attributed to the base-face offset: the apparent leak also occurred with face bias disabled.
- **Face-ID prototype:** With a separate different-face occluder, the prototype hid the line in a head-on case with a `+0.0001` displacement. In an oblique case at `+0.001`, a line appeared over gray when the prototype used its depth fallback; exact ray-based visibility was not verified. An ID-only version suppressed the line over a different face, but also lost exterior silhouette lines. With a same-face-ID occluder at `+0.08`, it leaked.
- **Limits:** The face-ID target was single-sampled, and multisampling behavior was not validated. The fixture could not test a silhouette against an unrelated background. These checks do not establish correct occlusion, a general failure rate or comparative performance.
