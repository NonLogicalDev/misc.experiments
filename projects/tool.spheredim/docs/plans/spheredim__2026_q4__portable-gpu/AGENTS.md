# Scope and guidance

Follow [owner authorization](OWNERS.md), the user's global instructions, and current project instructions. Port 360DVO to execute on Apple and AMD GPUs without its CUDA-specific extensions, preserving full equirectangular input. Use layerctl to keep upstream provenance and the port layer reproducible. CPU is a validation reference; GPU execution is the goal. Validate native GPU behavior, kernel correctness, Blender-compatible output, and trajectory/image accuracy on existing diverse test scenes. Do not infer AMD support from Apple tests. Preserve existing SphereDim. The user authorizes pushing verified code and documentation upstream; private footage and derived report figures remain local. Record decisions and checks in the [active plan](theme-001-portable-tracking/active/plan-01%20Portable%20360DVO.md).

For Insta360 footage, preserve yaw while leveling the horizon, and exclude the operator before reconstruction. Keep masks aligned to the input video and report coverage and orientation limitations. Use app-free export where feasible; do not claim camera IMU orientation is a measured translation or drift-free ground truth.

Any upstream fork or modification must use layerctl with pinned source and a separate maintained patch layer. Keep layerctl external; do not vendor or submodule the tool. Reconstruction exports must preserve observed source pixels and explicit validity rather than fill or cosmetically warp unsupported content. See the Terra quality plan for source-observation, lens-provenance and validation requirements.

Use Terra subagents for cheap routine checks and bounded implementation tasks; keep research judgments and final integration with the owner. Preserve the separate viewer worker.

Keep the viewer free of repeated generic experimental/unvalidated disclaimers. Show plain execution status and concrete actionable problems; put provenance and validation caveats in Activity/details or reports. Improving output quality takes priority over warning copy.

The viewer is a generic consumer of a state file/directory. No recording-specific stages, artifact selection, filter decisions or run labels belong in viewer code; publish them through state producers. State changes must refresh without restarting the viewer. Publish completed candidate reconstructions for inspection even when quality metrics regress. Keep their resolution, iteration count and actual execution status in state. Candidate availability and the measured quality incumbent are separate decisions; do not hide completed work until it beats the incumbent.

The operator explicitly authorizes treating the two physical Insta360 lenses as a rigid camera rig for SfM and comparing accuracy. Preserve synchronized timestamps and one body pose per timestamp with two fixed camera extrinsics; per-lens spherical images are actual sensor observations, not virtual perspective extraction. Native spherical tracking remains required. Avoid imposing a shared optical center or guessed surface radius as reconstruction truth. Keep measured source support, alignment uncertainty, operator exclusion and descriptor guard masks distinct. The exclusion around a stitch is not necessarily a physically uncovered region.

Keep total task storage below 15 GB and non-reconstruction support below 2 GB, with a 35 GiB free-space reserve. Use Typst for the requested scientific PDF; preserve source and evidence, and verify rendered pages.

The research report evaluates accuracy and speed together for reconstruction from average-quality footage recorded with one consumer 360 camera, beginning with stitched panoramas. Treat its physical two-lens rig as an alternative within that single-device scope. Use moderately formal, readable prose with purposeful diagrams and graphs; include operator masking and Apple/AMD acceleration attempts, distinguishing measurements from intended portability. The operator explicitly requests direct LAN viewer access without pairing or login.
