# Training-view edge-profile diagnostic

2026-11-15. This is a CPU-only examination of the existing 10,000-step raw training-camera audit. No scene was retrained and no evaluation-time alignment was applied. The three fixed source/render crops are doorway frame 930 cam0, fabric frame 1005 cam0, and window blinds frame 1500 cam0. They are selected regions, not a random or representative sample. The full audit verified 26 retained GT/mask/render controls exactly and the original checkpoint hashes; raw eval excludes the trainer's per-camera appearance correction. See `rig-training-view-diverse-20261115/audit.json`, `profile-analysis-v2.json`, and `profile-regions-v2.png` beside the recording.

Each profile samples 17 positions along a source Sobel edge normal inside an eroded valid mask. Source Canny thresholds are 50/150; a source-only selection requires at least 20 grayscale levels of endpoint contrast and limits negative derivative. The width spans 10–90% of cumulative **positive** sampled gradient, and signed displacement is the render-minus-source gradient centroid along that normal. Render/source contrast is the ratio of differences between the two three-pixel end windows. Each region began with at most 600 regularly selected source edge candidates; the table reports the selected source steps with positive render contrast. All selected profiles in these three crops had positive render contrast. The report also retains 10th and 90th percentiles, selection counts and input hashes.

These profile widths are **not an optical point-spread measurement**. Multiple nearby edges, texture, illumination, saturation, a mismatched surface and the 17-pixel window can move the centroid or inflate the width. Strong source-selected step edges can survive while fine periodic texture disappears, so a median contrast near or above one does not imply the source's detail is retained. The blinded window crop visibly loses fine exterior and blind detail despite its selected-step median contrast ratio of 1.10. Its valid-region gradient RMS is 0.687 of source. The doorway crop's selected strong profiles have similar width and contrast, while source and render fine structures differ. Fabric has higher selected-step contrast (1.31) and a gradient RMS ratio near one, yet the crop shows different fold/texture structure; higher gradient energy is not evidence of matching detail.

For a separate displacement diagnostic, compare signed x/y Sobel gradients in the same eroded mask, and search integer offsets within ±4 pixels. The best correlation is at `(0, 0)` for each crop: 0.867 doorway, 0.811 fabric and 0.711 window. This supports coarse local alignment in these particular regions, not absence of local warping or a correct camera trajectory; large edges can dominate and the search does not fit a spatial deformation. Neither this diagnostic nor the profile statistics distinguish calibration error from scene representation, view-dependent appearance, exposure or source blur.

Synthetic checks on an isolated step verify the implementation distinguishes a Gaussian blur, a two-pixel translation and a half-contrast change, and rejects an invalid mask. These are implementation controls, not calibration of real images. The tool is `tools/native-sfm/analyze_edge_profiles.py`; `test_analyze_edge_profiles.py` holds the checks. It writes only a small JSON report and crop sheet, with a 20 MiB cap.

## Source audit of the companion research note

- [Seam360GS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20080) models dual-fisheye lens-center separation and angular distortion in its stitched-panorama setting. This supports testing those effects, not treating the present estimated baseline as known metric truth.
- [Self-Calibrating Gaussian Splatting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09563) jointly optimizes camera and distortion parameters with the representation. Its [official implementation](https://github.com/denghilbert/Self-Cali-GS) presents cubemap sampling as an option for wider fields of view. The companion note's unqualified statement that the implementation uses cubemap rendering should be read as referring to that wider-FOV route, not every possible use.
- [Mip-Splatting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16493) describes a 3D frequency constraint and 2D Mip filter for sampling-rate artifacts. It does not establish that reducing this project's filter will restore missing texture.
- [Google's indoor NeRF account](https://research.google/blog/reconstructing-indoor-spaces-with-nerf/) supports the companion note's statements about dense capture, per-image latent appearance and exposure conditioning; it does not describe Scaniverse's optimizer.
- The companion note's `scaniverse.com/support` link currently redirects to a general [Niantic Capture page](https://www.nianticspatial.com/products/capture) that does not substantiate its detailed capture-guide claims. An older indexed support page contained those tips, but the current redirect should not be presented as live supporting evidence without finding a current primary guide.
