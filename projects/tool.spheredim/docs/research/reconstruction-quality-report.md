# Reconstruction quality report

Updated 2026-11-16. The best measured reconstruction so far is the 7,000-step continuation of the dense physical-lens rig model. It improves the pooled error over the 6,000-step model, but the reconstruction remains visibly soft in fine details and has not met the requested twofold quality target. This report is a current decision record; update its evidence and incumbent when a new trial is verified.

## Quality target and measurement

The fixed target anchor is the dense 6,000-step model: achieve at least a 50% reduction in pooled spherical weighted mean squared RGB error at matched source captures, and show visible fine-detail improvement. Every new candidate must also be compared directly with the current 7,000-step incumbent. For the current masks and views, half of the 6,000-step error is 0.00193296 on the 36 held-out views and 0.00413972 on the 10 selected training views. Numerical gains alone do not prove recovered detail.

The table pools squared RGB error across all common valid pixels using spherical area weights before dividing by total weight. Lower is better; these are not averages of per-image PSNR. Both models in each pair use the same source image and mask intersection without fitted image alignment or appearance correction. Each reconstruction is rendered at its own estimated pose for the same physical capture. The 36 splat-held-out images contributed to SfM, so they are not independent pose ground truth. The 10 selected training captures are a diagnostic, not a representative sample of every training view.

## ERP seam correction

The native ERP renderer clipped seam-crossing splats. The disabled [periodic raster layer](../../vendor/spirula-studio/layers/0006-erp-periodic-raster/) passed 16 GPU forward comparisons against CPU references and 28 ordinary-case finite-difference gradient checks; non-ERP pinhole controls also passed the stated tolerance. A complete source-built RGB trainer included all 82 affected variants and passed the full registry audit. On the same unmodified 6,000-step checkpoint, held-out spherical MSE fell 0.19% overall and 19.72% in the outer 16-pixel seam bands, while the 10 training views rose 0.69% overall and 14.20% in those bands. The correction remains disabled: fixture correctness does not establish a reconstruction-quality gain, and the separate meshing path, CUDA, and AMD remain unverified. See the [detailed seam note](spirula-erp-periodic-layer.md) and [checkpoint comparison](/private/tmp/spheredim-seam-eval-6k/report.json).

## Selecting frames by sharpness and scene change

The new selector scans forward from the last accepted physical-lens pair. It compares sharpness on valid scene pixels and measures matched feature movement after compensating camera rotation. A small penalty discourages choosing the very next timestamp; it decreases with elapsed footage time and resets after acceptance. A nearby frame can still win when it provides more useful information. Frames without enough measurable overlap cannot qualify simply because their images differ.

The corrected CPU pilot selected 283 of the existing 412 paired timestamps in 31.72 seconds. This is a selection from the existing approximately 6 Hz input pool, not a scan of every original video frame. The final selected gaps have median 0.167 seconds and maximum 0.334 seconds. Nearest preliminary camera-center distance was slightly lower than uniform-time selection at the same count: p95 0.179 versus 0.187 scene units, maximum 0.261 versus 0.280. These distances describe camera spacing, not surface coverage or splat quality.

The reconstruction comparison preserves the original 18 evaluation timestamps and both lenses at each one. Eleven selected timestamps belong to that evaluation set, leaving 272 training timestamps. Fresh SfM uses their union: 290 timestamps, 580 lens images and 23,696 matching pairs. All 580 images registered into one model with 56,620 points. The successful retry took 47.87 seconds, but reused a workspace after the first attempt failed on a CSV header line ending; it is not a clean runtime comparison against the earlier dense run. The 6,000-step splat completed in 345.43 seconds, including final output and evaluation, with the original evaluation pixels, reviewed masks, 300,000-Gaussian cap and refinement schedule. It contains 290,999 Gaussians and is selectable in the viewer.

## Isolating frame selection from the new camera solution

The selected-frame control retained the original dense camera poses and all 74,984 initial points, while training on the same 272 selected timestamps. All 36 evaluation images, their order, reviewed masks and prepared pose rows were checked against the dense baseline. It used the same 6,000-step schedule and saved a compact final checkpoint. Processing completed in 366.65 seconds, producing 296,396 Gaussians; the result is available as **Selected frames · fixed poses · 6,000-step splat** in the viewer.

Spherical MSE is 0.00380554, 1.56% below dense 6k but 6.60% above the 7k incumbent. Raw pixel MSE is 0.00395541. Retaining the original geometry and seed reduces error 70.52% compared with the fresh adaptive reconstruction. This separates the frame subset from the combined geometry/initial-point change; it does not distinguish camera error from seed quality. The [fixed-pose comparison](</Users/nonlogical/Downloads/SphereDim-VID_20181001_170023_00_104/adaptive-fixed-poses-6000-20261116/comparison-to-dense-6000.json>) and [matched detail crops](</Users/nonlogical/Downloads/SphereDim-VID_20181001_170023_00_104/adaptive-fixed-poses-6000-20261116/heldout-detail-comparison-20261116/heldout-detail.png>) show that the small numerical gain does not recover the clock markings or blind slats. The twofold fidelity target remains unmet.

The next selection audit measures observed landmark support, not just camera spacing. Among 58,278 landmarks visible at two or more valid training timestamps in the original model, the earlier selection retains such support for 42,531. A deliberately strict feasibility probe retaining three timestamps for every originally three-view-or-longer track cannot remove any frame: every timestamp supports at least one exactly-three-view track. That is a consequence of the chosen constraint, not evidence that every source frame is indispensable. The probe neither establishes a production threshold nor demonstrates a detail gain. The useful next experiment seeks sharper replacements in the original 30 fps footage while retaining the established spatial coverage and camera solution. The [coverage audit](</Users/nonlogical/Downloads/SphereDim-VID_20181001_170023_00_104/landmark-coverage-audit-verified-20261116/audit.json>) and [constraint feasibility report](</Users/nonlogical/Downloads/SphereDim-VID_20181001_170023_00_104/coverage-selection-verified-20261116.json>) preserve the measurements.
