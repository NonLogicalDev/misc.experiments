# Reconstruction quality report

Updated 2026-11-16. The lowest measured spherical error so far comes from continuing the original model to 15,000 steps. It is 2.66% below the original 7k result and 10.11% below the fixed 6k anchor; the native-SfM 7k model is close behind. Clock markings and blind slats remain soft; the requested twofold fidelity improvement has not been achieved. This report is a current decision record; update its evidence and incumbent when a new trial is verified.

## Quality target and measurement

The fixed target anchor is the dense 6,000-step model: achieve at least a 50% reduction in pooled spherical weighted mean squared RGB error at matched source captures, and show visible fine-detail improvement. Every new candidate must also be compared with the original 7,000-step continuation and the current 15k incumbent. For the current masks and views, half of the 6,000-step error is 0.00193296 on the 36 held-out views and 0.00413972 on the 10 selected training views. Numerical gains alone do not prove recovered detail.

## Capacity and where detail receives it

The original dense 6k run reached its 300,000-Gaussian cap by step 3,401. The fresh 2048 trial keeps its seed, poses and split, increases the cap to one million and growth factor to 1.10, and retains the original 6,000-step training and refinement boundaries. Actual growth stopped at 921,760 Gaussians before step 4,000; the exported PLY retains 887,774. The run completed in 999.3 seconds and produced about 393 MB before duplicate references were hardlinked. The jointly changed resolution and growth/capacity form a candidate quality recipe rather than an isolated causal test.

The [CDC-GS paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b91cc0a242e6518ee731f74e82b2eebd-Abstract-Conference.html) offers a relevant next question: does reconstruction error direct too much capacity away from fine textures? Its method uses wavelet information from the source images and neighboring Gaussian density to decide where to add or remove primitives. The pinned trainer already has a simpler alternative, `densify_loss_map_mode=edge_aware`, which allocates from masked source-image edges while leaving the RGB objective unchanged. This is not CDC-GS. The controlled comparison below tests that existing option; its failure does not test the paper’s method.

[Niantic's public reconstruction guide](https://www.nianticspatial.com/docs/nsdk/how-to/ar/gaussian_splat/) recommends full-resolution capture at 15 FPS, smooth motion and multiple viewing angles. Its quality levels add processing rounds, corresponding to Enhance in Scaniverse. The guide does not disclose the optimizer, Gaussian allocation strategy or a native spherical input route, so those details remain unknown. It supports preserving useful observations and source detail, rather than attributing the product's quality to one undocumented trick.
