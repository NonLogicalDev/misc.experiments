# Niantic and Google reconstruction methods

Research checked 2026-11-15. These are relevant approaches, not an exhaustive ranking, and none has been validated on this recording. SphereDim requires full equirectangular input, no extracted perspective or cube views, and GPU execution on Apple and AMD.

## Niantic depth and mesh research

Niantic states that its production reconstruction uses depth and surface constraints informed by [SimpleRecon (ECCV 2022), DoubleTake (ECCV 2024), and MVSAnywhere (CVPR 2025)](https://www.nianticspatial.com/blog/geometrically-accurate-reconstruction). The production pipeline is not claimed to be identical to those public implementations. The useful direction is to supervise geometry during splat optimization and validate the resulting surface separately, rather than assume visually appealing Gaussian opacity is an accurate mesh.

- [SimpleRecon](https://nianticlabs.github.io/simplerecon/) predicts multi-view depth and fuses it for 3D reconstruction. [DoubleTake](https://github.com/nianticlabs/doubletake) uses its own incrementally or offline built geometry to guide a subsequent depth prediction, which can reduce ambiguity but can also propagate an incorrect initial surface.
- [MVSAnywhere](https://nianticlabs.github.io/mvsanywhere/) predicts multi-view depth with adaptive depth ranges. Its [released implementation](https://github.com/nianticlabs/mvsanywhere) includes a depth-regularized Gaussian trainer (`regsplatfacto`) and TSDF fusion for mesh extraction. Depth and mesh are tied to input pose scale; ordinary monocular SfM does not establish metric scale.
- The released MVSAnywhere [cost volume](https://github.com/nianticlabs/mvsanywhere/blob/main/src/mvsanywhere/modules/cost_volume.py) uses `BackprojectDepth` and `Project3D`, and excludes samples near image edges. Its [geometry code](https://github.com/nianticlabs/mvsanywhere/blob/main/src/mvsanywhere/utils/geometry_utils.py) backprojects using inverse camera intrinsics and projects by dividing by z. That is a perspective model, not a valid full equirectangular ray model. Using full ERP input requires spherical projection/backprojection, longitude wrapping, spherical boundary-aware filtering and validation of the learned network under its changed input distribution. Retraining or adaptation may be necessary; this is not established by swapping the camera equations alone.
- The provided [MVSAnywhere environment](https://github.com/nianticlabs/mvsanywhere/blob/main/environment.yml) requires CUDA and `gsplat`. Apple/AMD GPU execution is not demonstrated by these releases; porting their relevant operators and dependencies is a separate task. Their [MVSAnywhere](https://github.com/nianticlabs/mvsanywhere/blob/main/LICENSE), [DoubleTake](https://github.com/nianticlabs/doubletake/blob/main/LICENSE), and [SimpleRecon](https://github.com/nianticlabs/simplerecon/blob/main/LICENSE) licenses restrict use to non-commercial purposes and discuss patent rights. Review each exact license and model terms before incorporating code or weights. We have not downloaded or used these models.

## Google-associated work

- [DRGSplat (BMVC 2026)](https://research.google/pubs/drgsplat-depth-regularized-3d-gaussian-splatting/) regularizes rendered depth with monocular depth, surface normals and uncertainty-aware flatness while retaining volumetric Gaussian primitives. The authors report geometry gains on ETH3D; those numbers are their perspective-data benchmarks, not measured SphereDim results. The Google publication page says code will be released; this research pass did not establish an available official implementation, native ERP, or Apple/AMD GPU support. A spherical analogue would need correctly scaled, confidence-weighted depth and seam-aware normals; false depth priors could make geometry worse.
- [Splat-SLAM](https://github.com/google-research/Splat-SLAM) is a Google-affiliated RGB-only SLAM research project combining optical-flow pose tracking, a global pose graph and deformable Gaussian mapping. Its top-level repository is Apache-2.0 and archived; its own instructions require CUDA, DROID backends and a forward-facing rasterizer. Check third-party and model licenses separately. It is a design reference for pose-graph correction and map consistency, not an implementation ready for native spherical Apple/AMD execution. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16544).
- [3D Gaussian Splatting as Markov Chain Monte Carlo](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/93be245fce00a9bb2333c17ceae4b732-Abstract-Conference.html) (NeurIPS 2024) includes Google Research and Google DeepMind authors. Its stochastic relocation and controlled population are relevant densification methods, but this is not evidence that changing densification alone fixes erroneous poses, operator masks, or mesh geometry. The current Spirula Studio training log reports dead-Gaussian relocation; equivalence to the paper’s complete MCMC strategy is unverified. Compare implementation and settings before assuming the approach is missing.
- [Zip-NeRF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Barron_Zip-NeRF_Anti-Aliased_Grid-Based_Neural_Radiance_Fields_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf) (Google Research, ICCV 2023) is a neural radiance field, not a Gaussian splat or mesh algorithm. It motivates anti-aliased sampling, but a `360` scene benchmark does not itself mean a native equirectangular 360 camera model. The public implementation and GPU portability require separate audit before use.

## Proposed tests

1. Finish the controlled pose comparison already running with identical full-panorama images, masks, seed, and held-out frames. Do not attribute changes to depth or mesh methods.
2. Against known-geometry Blender scenes and real selected frames, produce confidence-filtered spherical multi-view depth with radial distance and seam-aware sampling. Compare depth and normals to independent ground truth where available; avoid treating learned relative depth as measurement.
3. Once validated, compare identical splat runs with and without one native spherical depth or normal constraint. Keep operator masks out of all losses, hold inputs and pose set constant, and measure masked, area-weighted novel-frame appearance and geometry separately.
4. Fuse only multiview-consistent depths into a surface with an explicit confidence/coverage representation; report unsupported areas as holes. Measure surface error and completeness on synthetic ground truth and check held-out reprojection on the real capture. Do not call a mesh high quality based on component counts or photometric appearance alone.
5. Implement or port GPU kernels using a portable path and test actual Apple and AMD devices; neither the papers nor a successful Apple run demonstrates AMD compatibility.

## Further renderer and representation audit

- **[Radiance Meshes (CVPR 2026)](https://half-potato.gitlab.io/rm/)** is a Google-affiliated collaboration that optimizes volumetric radiance carried by Delaunay tetrahedra and renders the field through rasterization or ray tracing. This is a tetrahedral volume representation; the tetrahedra are not automatically a conventional accurate boundary mesh. The [original trainer](https://github.com/half-potato/radiance_meshes) documents NVIDIA GPU/Linux and warns that fisheye training quality is questionable. Its top-level license is [MIT](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/half-potato/radiance_meshes/master/LICENSE); individual submodules and dependencies need separate review. The author's separate [Rust/wgpu renderer and differentiable training backend](https://github.com/half-potato/rmesh_rs) claims support for Vulkan or Metal, but this audit did not establish a top-level license, complete end-to-end training, ERP input, Apple execution, or AMD execution. The [Vulkan viewer](https://github.com/half-potato/vkrm) and [WebGPU viewer](https://github.com/half-potato/webrm) are MIT according to their repositories, but viewer portability does not prove portable training. A promising later experiment is to pin and audit the Rust backend, its licenses and training completeness, then implement and test whole-sphere ray generation and seam handling before measuring it against the current native-ERP baseline. Do not replace the standard PLY/mesh outputs with tetrahedral data without an explicit conversion and geometry validation.
- **[EVER (ICCV 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01804)** is Google-affiliated exact emission-only volumetric ray rendering with ellipsoids; the paper discusses distortion and defocus, not measured support for this ERP pipeline. The [Google repository](https://github.com/google/ever) supplies rendering code under Apache-2.0. Its [separate training repository](https://github.com/half-potato/ever_training) requires CUDA/OptiX and publishes a [research and evaluation-only Gaussian-Splatting license](https://github.com/half-potato/ever_training/blob/master/LICENSE.md). Neither Apple/AMD portability nor spherical-image training is established. Rasterization artefact comparisons or renderer mathematics are research leads; don't conflate renderer and trainer terms.
- **[SMERF (SIGGRAPH 2024)](https://smerf-3d.github.io/)** is Google Research/DeepMind work on hierarchical partitioning and distillation for streamable real-time radiance fields. It is neither a Gaussian splat nor an explicit surface mesh, and its [released training setup](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/smerf) specifies NVIDIA CUDA and cuDNN. Its browser/commodity-device demos do not prove Apple/AMD training support or native ERP training. Scene partitioning and distillation are later scaling options, not remedies for our current erroneous poses or mask.
- **[BakedSDF (SIGGRAPH 2023)](https://bakedsdf.github.io/)** is Google Research work fitting a hybrid neural volume/surface, extracting triangles, and optimizing view-dependent appearance on them. Its triangle output is more directly aligned with the requested conventional mesh than a radiance tetrahedral volume, but its appearance and geometry still need separate validation. The original project page links paper and demos but no official implementation; native ERP and Apple/AMD training are not established. It suggests testing an explicitly regularized spherical surface objective once reliable depths and poses exist, not presenting a derived splat mesh as ground truth.
- **[3DGRT and 3DGUT](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/3dgrut) are NVIDIA work, not Google.** 3DGRT ray-traces Gaussian volumes; 3DGUT approximates nonlinear projection using sigma points and supports distorted cameras and time-varying effects in its formulation. The official repository's documented training requires CUDA; its top-level license is Apache-2.0 and third-party terms differ. A separate Vulkan viewer exists, but does not prove native ERP differentiable training or cross-platform kernel support. For spherical projection a port must explicitly test longitude wrap, pole coverage, large footprints, visibility ordering and gradients; claiming arbitrary-camera mathematics does not establish those properties.
- **[Eulerian Gaussian Splatting (CVPR 2026)](https://euleriansplatting.github.io/)**, a Harvard/Google/Google DeepMind collaboration, replaces heuristic primitive relocation with samples from an optimized multiscale probability density. This is a candidate controlled densification experiment after stable poses and masks; its published results do not establish improved accuracy on our footage, native ERP support, or Apple/AMD execution. The [released training repository](https://github.com/euleriansplatting/eulerian-splatting) specifies CUDA 12.1, an NVIDIA H200, and tiny-cuda-nn, and its top level does not show a license file; audit code, terms, submodules and the spherical camera path before any integration.

Across these sources, renderer speed, image quality, metric geometry, surface completeness, and real camera pose are different claims. The present comparison should establish if better-connected spherical SfM poses improve this recording before introducing a new representation or unvalidated geometric prior.

## Direct spherical references and released-code constraints

These are independent research, not Niantic or Google publications, but provide stronger comparisons for SphereDim's native-panorama constraint:

- [ODGS-SLAM (CVPR 2026)](https://odgs-slam.github.io/) jointly tracks and maps using a Gaussian scene from equirectangular RGB or RGB-D. Its [released implementation](https://github.com/odgs-slam/odgs-slam) documents RTX 4090/CUDA evaluation and a non-commercial academic-research license inherited from MonoGS. It is a reference for direct spherical tracking and a potential port study, not an Apple/AMD-ready backend. The later PanoGS-SLAM preprint calls itself the first panoramic Gaussian SLAM system; ODGS-SLAM's earlier CVPR publication makes that priority claim unsuitable to repeat without further investigation.
- [SPaGS (EGSR 2025)](https://github.com/nerficg-project/SPaGS) implements full spherical panorama splatting with an MIT top-level license and a CUDA backend. Audit any framework and dependency licenses independently. Compare its 3D ray evaluation and training loss with our existing spherical implementation before deciding whether a wgpu port would address an observed rendering failure.
- [UniTriSplat (ECCV 2026)](https://github.com/yipengzhu0809/UniTriSplat) has now released code for equirectangular inputs, equal-area HEALPix splatting and spherical-neighborhood SSIM. Its verified configuration is NVIDIA CUDA. Its repository says the top-level MIT license applies to the authors' original code only; files inherited from 3DGS and third-party submodules carry their own terms. HEALPix rasterization and SSIM are useful spherical-loss experiments, but its scores are not evidence of quality or portability on this capture.
- [PanoGS-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17387) describes sphere-area-aware direct optimization and depth-guided insertion. Its authors say code will be released, and they cite BiFuse for a depth component; inspect that component's projections separately before claiming the full pipeline meets the no-extracted-views requirement. An available abstract or source for a single component does not validate the complete pipeline.
- [Seam360GS (ICCV 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20080) targets the double-lens stitching distortions of consumer 360 cameras and learns optical-center offsets and a smooth angular distortion field from stitched panoramas while fitting Gaussians. Its authors evaluate an Insta360 indoor dataset, but those benchmark results do not establish that seam distortion causes our blur. Their training renderer forms front- and back-lens contributions separately before concatenating a panorama; whether that satisfies the user's no-multiple-views rule requires explicit interpretation. A single-panorama ray-level observation model is a possible research adaptation, not a validated equivalent. GPU portability and published code are unverified in this audit.
- [GeoGaussian (ECCV 2024)](https://github.com/yanyan-li/GeoGaussian), with a Google-affiliated author, studies geometry-aware initialization, surface-normal alignment and densification for weakly textured indoor walls. Its reported pipeline expects plane-aware points and normals from separate SLAM; its repository says the Gaussian-Splatting license applies, and its sample code explicitly uses CUDA. Native ERP and Apple/AMD are not established. Plane priors may bias non-planar furniture and clutter, so evaluate them only with independent geometry truth and label prior-driven regions.
- Google DeepMind's [LoGeR (ECCV 2026)](https://loger-project.github.io/) studies long-context point and pose reconstruction by combining short-window attention with learned persistent memory. The [linked repository](https://github.com/junyi42/LoGeR) describes itself as a reimplementation and says complete code and models await approval. No equirectangular camera support, Apple/AMD path, or usable license for that reimplementation was established. Its long-video memory ideas do not make its perspective-trained model a native spherical tracker.

## Scaniverse quality reference

Scaniverse Classic is evidence that good-looking local splats are possible on a phone, not a reason to lower SphereDim's quality bar. Niantic's [March 2024 release notes](https://www.nianticspatial.com/capture/scaniverse-release-notes) state that its new iOS Gaussian-splat mode processed on device. Its August 2024 notes describe improved distant backgrounds and thin-detail reconstruction. Niantic's [current product FAQ](https://www.nianticspatial.com/en/pricing) says Classic still generates splats on device; it separately describes a newer cloud workflow and says its 360-video splats are processed via Scaniverse Web. These documents do not disclose enough of the Classic trainer or renderer to recreate its algorithm or attribute its quality to any specific technique. Niantic's open [SPZ library](https://github.com/nianticlabs/spz) describes storage and compression, not its on-device training pipeline. The [published research pipeline](https://www.nianticspatial.com/blog/geometrically-accurate-reconstruction) references depth and surface research but should not be represented as the exact Classic implementation.

Niantic's current [capture guide for 360 cameras](https://www.nianticspatial.com/docs/scaniverse/360camera/) recommends well-lit even lighting, stable slow movement, overlapping routes, revisiting the start, avoiding very close surfaces and stitch-line artefacts, and varying camera height. For an X5 it suggests 8K/30 fps and an extended selfie stick; it recommends 1/500 shutter with automatic ISO indoors. Those are capture recommendations with exposure/noise tradeoffs, not a diagnosis of this recording. Its [phone guidance](https://www.nianticspatial.com/docs/scaniverse/techniques/) describes device position/orientation and optional LiDAR depth among captured data; it does not establish how Classic uses each signal for splat training. An iPhone capture and this stitched X5 panorama differ in optics, motion, scene coverage and available depth, so a quantitative head-to-head requires paired captures and matched views. Regardless, prioritize visible text and edges, frame-to-render alignment, low-texture walls, thin structures and novel-view quality as acceptance evidence, rather than claiming success because training ran or because a representation is portable.
