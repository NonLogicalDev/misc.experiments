# Sharpness with physical two-lens observations

2026-11-15. This supplements [the speed and quality review](splat-quality-speed-update.md); it does not infer Scaniverse's undisclosed optimizer. The immediate target is faithful detail at recorded camera positions and nearby positions, using each actual lens and its support mask. The 138-timestamp baseline already has separate physical camera centers; the 412-timestamp flow-mask dataset is a controlled candidate, not ground truth. Camera, filter, appearance, and source blur can all produce similar soft images, so these are hypotheses to separate rather than fixes to stack.

## 1. Diagnose alignment before adding model complexity

[Seam360GS (ICCV 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Shin_Seam360GS_Seamless_360deg_Gaussian_Splatting_from_Real-World_Omnidirectional_Images_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf) models dual-fisheye center separation and residual angular distortion to reduce stitching artifacts. It demonstrates why the two lens centers and residual calibration matter; its formulation targets stitched omnidirectional imagery and is not a replacement for our separate physical observations. Our known baseline is uncertain, so its fitted translation is not metric ground truth. [Self-Calibrating Gaussian Splatting (ICCV 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Deng_Self-Calibrating_Gaussian_Splatting_for_Large_Field-of-View_Reconstruction_ICCV_2025_paper.html) jointly optimizes lens distortion, poses, and Gaussians against original input pixels. The paper proposes cubemap resampling for wide fields of view; the [authors’ repository](https://github.com/denghilbert/Self-Cali-GS) also presents non-cubemap distortion optimization and cubemap sampling as an option. Compatibility with our native ERP, physical two-lens constraints is unverified; a flexible distortion field could absorb pose errors or scene geometry. Its result motivates a constrained test, not a direct port.

First stratify existing source-position residuals by lens, timestamp, angular distance from lens center, and overlap versus interior. Use only mutually supported, static pixels and geometric correspondences; compare paired-lens feature reprojection and edge displacement, retaining signed directions and spatial distributions. If residuals grow systematically with lens angle or differ by lens, test a small smooth per-lens angular correction on held-out tracks with fixed body poses and bounded amplitude; validate inverse mapping and periodic boundaries. If residuals instead correlate with timestamp, test bounded body-pose refinement while keeping the lens-to-rig transform fixed. Anchor the gauge and retain the orientation prior. Evaluate held-out tracks, independent timestamps, per-lens error and actual source-position images before considering a combined optimization. A photometric improvement alone cannot identify which variable was wrong.

## 2. Measure source detail and filtering separately

Compare each source image with the raw render at the *same physical center and orientation*, with the same support and operator mask. Keep exposure correction disabled in the evaluation image, and also save the trainer's appearance-corrected training prediction as a separate diagnostic. Record edge-spread width and contrast at selected window blinds, fabric, doorway and lens overlap, alongside area-weighted errors and original crops. Check nearby actual frames for motion blur and exposure changes before attributing lost detail to training. Edge location alone is insufficient: the existing audit found doorway edges can align while finer textures disappear. A reference image may itself be blurred, and sharpening a render by unsharp masking cannot recover unseen scene detail.

[Mip-Splatting (CVPR 2024)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yu_Mip-Splatting_Alias-free_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_CVPR_2024_paper.html) separates a 3D frequency constraint from a 2D pixel-footprint filter; it reports improvements under changed sampling rates, not universal source-view sharpening. Existing `primitive=mip` is not proven equivalent or ERP-correct. Audit the trainer's covariance floor, opacity compensation, and screen-space filter in forward *and* backward paths, and the viewer's separate renderer; a raw trainer image and viewer screenshot need not share the same filter. Synthetic periodic seam, pole, and subpixel tests should compare against spherical pixel-area integration at several resolutions. Then change one filter parameter or primitive at a time, keeping data, poses, splat cap, schedule and saved checkpoints fixed. Report source-view edge contrast and aliasing as well as shifted-view results: reducing a blur floor may make details sharper while introducing shimmer, holes or unstable gradients. The 2048 diagnostic's small gain at 16 timestamps and short training does not rule out a resolution effect, but it does not establish one either.

## 3. Separate illumination from geometry without hiding detail

[Google's indoor NeRF account](https://research.google/blog/reconstructing-indoor-spaces-with-nerf/?m=0) describes dense capture and separating changing appearance and exposure from scene representation. It is a NeRF system, not evidence of Scaniverse internals or a ready-to-run ERP splat implementation. Here the completed appearance-off 3k experiment was 31% faster but regressed on 23/36 PSNR and 30/36 SSIM views. Retain the existing appearance path for the next geometry comparison. Quantify raw versus appearance-corrected per-view residuals and edge contrast, including overlap between lenses at each timestamp. If a per-image model removes actual texture or explains geometric misalignment, test a restrained shared lens/exposure correction with explicit regularization, comparing both corrected training predictions and the uncorrected exported splat. Do not fit an evaluation-time per-image correction to make the result look better. Track all runtime, because a better appearance model can add backward and total cost.

## 4. Compare at real positions and control capture coverage

[Niantic Spatial’s current capture page](https://www.nianticspatial.com/products/capture) lists 360° cameras among supported inputs and describes splat outputs, but does not disclose a comparable optimizer or detailed capture guidance. The former Scaniverse support URL redirects to that page. This recording is fixed, but the lesson is testable: stratify the existing trajectory by baseline/parallax, angular coverage, blur and occlusion. Adding intermediate frames improves temporal sampling but does not create new viewpoints or recover detail absent from all frames. Preserve identical 138-timestamp test positions for the 138-versus-412 experiment; derive the denser rig's source times and candidate masks from the recorded review manifests, use matched physical camera intrinsics and orientation priors, and evaluate additional time-held-out images when available. The existing splat holdouts contributed to SfM, so they test rendering but do not independently validate pose. Assess source-position detail, nearby withheld timestamps, and trajectory-consistent offsets; an offset without a source photo is a visual stability test, not a quantitative fidelity measurement. Track mask coverage so a change cannot win by excluding hard pixels.

## Practical ordering

1. Run the inexpensive source/render, lens-angle, edge-width and appearance diagnostics on saved outputs; this establishes which failure is observable without retraining.
2. Complete the controlled comparison using 412 candidate timestamps (824 physical-lens observations) when the disk and GPU gates allow it, holding evaluation images, camera model and all other settings fixed. Include preprocessing, SfM, training and evaluation time; do not assume more frames mean faster or sharper results.
3. Depending on the residual signature, choose either a bounded calibration/pose experiment or a filter ablation, after their independent geometry and spherical-gradient tests. Compare same wall time and same splat cap as well as equal-step runs. Preserve the baseline; a 2–3× end-to-end speedup or superior sharpness is not established by any cited paper for Apple M2 Pro or AMD Vulkan.

## Dense 6k capacity and exposure audit

The completed [dense run log](/Users/nonlogical/Downloads/SphereDim-VID_20181001_170023_00_104/rig-dense-splat-6000-20261115/run.log) reports 300,000 live splats at step 3,401; refinement stops at 4,000. The final PLY contains 296,605 exported splats. Across opacity >0.05 splats, the maximum world-space axis projected by `atan(max_scale / distance) * 1024 / (2π)` has a median of 1.21–1.49 pixels at five sampled camera centers; 57–64% exceed one pixel. This is a conservative angular size approximation for all scene splats, not an occlusion-aware raster footprint or a measurement of a particular detail. The [training-camera audit](/Users/nonlogical/Downloads/SphereDim-VID_20181001_170023_00_104/rig-dense-splat-6000-20261115/training-view-audit/audit.json) reports gradient ratios 0.625–0.998 across ten cameras; these mix displacement and contrast as well as detail loss.

The [resolved configuration](/Users/nonlogical/Downloads/SphereDim-VID_20181001_170023_00_104/rig-dense-splat-6000-20261115/run/config.json) and prepared camera model specify 1024×512 ERP. Source inspection shows divisor 0 leaves that resolution unchanged, progressive resolution is off, and the 1,920-pixel loss-scale threshold resolves to a single scale for a 512-pixel minimum dimension. Uniform sampling and the configured batch policy choose one of 788 training images per step: 6,000 steps comprise seven complete view passes and 484 views of the next, approximately seven or eight visits per camera. RGB bilateral and PPISP appearance are enabled; geometry bilateral grids require depth or normals, which this prepared dataset lacks. Raw evaluation excludes appearance correction. Sampling frequency, representation capacity, pose, and renderer behavior remain competing causes of missing detail.

A proposed continuation uses the existing full-resume 6,000-step checkpoint for 1,000 more steps, with the 300,000 cap and no more refinement; it is not a clean extra-exposure-only test because other step-dependent behavior may vary. A reviewed command and hashes are in [the bounded plan](/private/tmp/spheredim-dense-continuation-7000-plan.json). `max_steps=6000` holds scheduled learning rates at their terminal values. Opacity and scale regularizers are explicitly zero because the original decay reached zero at step 6,000, while changing total iterations to 7,000 would otherwise reactivate them. A separate fresh cap-only experiment at 450,000 can test representation capacity; with the existing growth and stop schedule it would reach roughly 400,000, not necessarily the cap. Both need identical source-view crops, common-mask held-out metrics, disk and runtime checks; neither demonstrates that the target will be reached.
