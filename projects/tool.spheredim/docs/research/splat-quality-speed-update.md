# Sharper splats and faster training: research update

2026-11-15. This note ranks experiments for the current physical two-lens, native-ERP rig, not a claim that another system uses a particular internal algorithm. Published training times below are not predictions for Apple M2 Pro or AMD. The relevant objective is source-faithful detail at and near the track at a given **end-to-end wall time**, including preprocessing, and not just a faster iteration or higher training-view score.

## Current evidence and the optimization budget

The existing 138-timestamp rig experiment uses two physical-lens ERP observations per timestamp. The 3,000-step run took 104.66 s and the 10,000-step run 802 s on Apple M2 Pro; the latter improved independent spherical masked PSNR/SSIM on 36 splat-held-out views from 21.87 dB/0.782 to 23.54 dB/0.850, while one doorway view regressed and visible geometry loss remains. These views contributed to SfM and its seed; they are not independent pose ground truth. The schedules differ with total steps, so their time and quality difference is not a pure iteration-count experiment. Source detail, lens calibration, pose error, visibility, renderer, and representation may all matter. See [rig comparison](rig-sfm-comparison.md) and the [renderer audit](spirula-native-erp-audit.md).

A short `SS_PROFILE=1` sample of the pinned 2026.11.12 trainer on M2 Pro, 1024×512, 24,614 initial splats, 240 training cameras and 36 evaluation renders, recorded 100 training steps. The default bilateral-grid tile backward was 631 ms/100 dispatches, SSIM backward 516 ms/100, FPBO 403 ms/100, raster backward 363 ms/100, bilateral TV optimizer 299 ms/100, and raster forward 270 ms/136. GPU profile totals exceeded the observed device wait, so use these to identify candidates, **not** add them into a wall-time breakdown. The experimental `SS_BILAGRID_BWD=v2` was slower (17.56 versus 16.15 s total on the short profile); it is not a speedup. These early, low-splat measurements need repetition at later population and resolution. Logs: `/private/tmp/spheredim-kernels-100.log`, `/private/tmp/spheredim-kernels-v2-100.log`.

## Ranked experiments

### 1. Reduce expensive per-pixel work while preserving fine detail

[TurboGS (ICML 2026)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/dong26k.html) combines error-guided tile/pixel selection, sparse normalized cross-correlation (NCC), error-driven density control and a Hessian-informed/Adam hybrid. Authors report up to 10× relative to their vanilla baseline on standard benchmark scenes; that result is for their complete method and benchmark, not a prediction for this renderer. Its appeal here is specific: sparse spatial loss and raster work might reduce **SSIM backward and raster backward**, and a different appearance policy might eliminate work in **bilateral-grid backward and TV**. Sparsifying loss alone does not guarantee a faster rasterizer: tiles, full-image setup, SSIM neighborhoods, memory traffic and FPBO may still run in full. Its reported hardware could not be established from the accessible abstract; implementation/platform portability is unverified. Do not import the reported multiplier.

A completed 3,000-step appearance-off comparison ran 31% faster but regressed in PSNR on 23/36 and SSIM on 30/36 views; it is not a candidate for promotion on this evidence. Before approximate sparse sampling, prioritize an exact-zero gradient-tile skip: an estimated 45–51% of fully masked tiles present an opportunity, but this is an inferred upper bound on candidate tiles, not a measured speedup. Prove full loss and gradient equivalence with mask-neighborhood support, including SSIM halos and longitude/pole boundaries; profile actual skipped work and end-to-end time. Then test a small staged ablation rather than porting all four components, with identical images, masks, poses, splits, seed, save/eval schedule, and raw uncorrected evaluation. Appearance-off may save time yet bake exposure differences into geometry or reduce view consistency; PPISP is not automatically cheaper or faithfully represented by a raw PLY. Profile the actual selected implementation. Next implement a Vulkan tile selection prototype with a fixed coverage floor plus residual-based sampling, periodic full-frame refresh, and importance-aware normalization. Ensure every valid physical-lens region and difficult doorway has a chance of selection; compare its gradients against a dense CPU/GPU reference and record selected fractions. An NCC experiment must preserve periodic longitude, pole policy, invalid-mask footprint, neighborhood normalization and backward invariance. Compare with ordinary masked SSIM at **equal wall time and equal source coverage**, with per-frame, edge and seam metrics. This is an approximation and can bias training toward moving-object edges or poorly posed frames; hard error alone is not evidence of missing geometry.

### 2. Allocate Gaussians using structure and multiview support

[FastGS (CVPR 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Ren_FastGS_Training_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_in_100_Seconds_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf) uses multiview-consistent densification/pruning and a compact projected bounding box. Its experiments used an NVIDIA RTX 4090; the paper acknowledges an extreme speed/quality tradeoff and reports per-backbone gains, not a guarantee for spherical scenes. Its compact-box contribution has a direct hypothesis for our **raster work** and splat/tile intersections, while controlling population also affects **FPBO** and subsequent steps. However ERP seam-wrapped and polar footprints make a planar box port unsafe. The existing audit identifies a possible seam coverage defect; fix and independently validate periodic footprint handling before using tighter bounds as an optimization. Compact boxes may cut useful tails if thresholds/gradients change.

[SAD-GS (SIGGRAPH 2026)](https://vcai.mpi-inf.mpg.de/projects/SAD-GS/) uses multiscale image structure to guide anisotropic splitting rather than merely adding identical children; its [authors' code](https://github.com/LinjieLyu/SADGS) is a conventional COLMAP/benchmark implementation, and Apple/AMD ERP support has not been established. Its motivation fits the missing thin edges, but extra image analysis and dynamic split work can cost more than it saves. Compare a structure-guided split against the existing densifier at the **same splat cap and wall time**; assess edge alignment against actual source detail, not gradient magnitude alone. Use the two physical cameras' valid observations and robust multiview consistency; never infer consistency through occluded, operator-masked or unsupported pixels. Track retained/pruned counts, splat sizes, tiles touched, FPBO and backward timings, and quality at nearby as well as training positions. False high-frequency texture, seams and pose errors can misdirect splits.

[Faster-GS (CVPR 2026)](https://github.com/nerficg-project/faster-gaussian-splatting) is a useful implementation study for fused optimizers, filtering and efficient scheduling. Its official backend explicitly requires an NVIDIA GPU and CUDA; it is not a ready Apple/AMD replacement. Before implementing any equivalent, inspect the pinned Spirula path to establish whether it already does the same operation. Test one mathematical change at a time and compare updates and gradients on fixed inputs before GPU timing.

### 3. Improve geometric consistency before relying on more iterations

[DirectFisheye-GS (CVPR 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Yang_DirectFisheye-GS_Enabling_Native_Fisheye_Input_in_Gaussian_Splatting_with_Cross-View_CVPR_2026_paper.html) trains directly on fisheye images and groups overlapping views for joint constraints to reduce distorted-edge floaters. Its [official implementation](https://github.com/Metaverse-AI-Lab-THU/DirectFisheye-GS) uses CUDA and COLMAP fisheye intrinsics, and declares research/noncommercial terms; it is not a drop-in Vulkan ERP or rigid two-camera rig trainer. The research idea is transferable: choose correlated physical-lens observations using measured shared tracks and visibility, then compare joint/alternating updates against random single-view updates at equal time. Do not manufacture perspective faces or give the two actual lenses a common optical center. Keep a fixed calibrated relative rig and one body pose per timestamp.

Joint camera/scene optimization can reduce double edges but can also move cameras to explain exposure, lens miscalibration, rolling shutter or erroneous correspondences. Start with known-motion synthetic spherical rigs, anchored gauge, fixed lens extrinsics, bounded pose updates, reliable cross-view correspondences and the existing soft orientation factors; evaluate on held-out feature tracks and adjacent-frame motion, not photometric training loss alone. Then try one localized real doorway interval with the original pose set preserved and report residuals and any degradation elsewhere. Sensor orientation is a prior, not translation truth. In the current renderer, real ERP depth/normal supervision is explicitly rejected; adding it requires validated radial range, visibility, seam/pole behavior and masked gradients, not merely enabling a flag. Sparse multiview depth constraints must abstain in low-parallax, occluded or uncertain regions and report accuracy versus coverage. No metric-scale claim follows from the uncertain rig baseline.

## Measurement and stop conditions

- Freeze inputs, masks, exact PTS, poses, seed, split, source resolution, appearance model, trainer settings, and versioned executable. Test at multiple seeds if a result is close. Save raw training and held-out images, no fitted per-frame evaluation alignment or color correction, and inspect the same doorway, thin structures, seam, and nearby positions. Report spherical-area-weighted PSNR/SSIM, source-relative edge localization, per-view regressions, coverage and splat counts alongside images. Splat-held-out frames still used for SfM do not independently validate poses.
- Time decode/export, pose/mask preparation, trainer, checkpoint and evaluation separately and together. Compare equal-quality time and equal-wall-time quality against the same baseline, with GPU synchronization and warm-up. Profile early and late, at actual resolution and population. A 2–3× goal means 0.5–0.33× baseline end-to-end time **at the chosen quality**, and sharper output must also be demonstrated; neither follows from per-kernel speedup.
- Verify Vulkan/MoltenVK numerics and mask/periodic gradients on Apple first, then separately run AMD Vulkan. CUDA papers are algorithm references, not evidence of AMD or Apple support. Preserve the two original physical-lens observations and source support; no virtual perspective extraction and no invented unsupported detail.

## Already available settings to test before a larger port

The verified 10,000-step configuration uses `primitive=3dgs`, `progressive_resolution=false`, and `view_sampling=uniform`. These make two low-integration experiments possible, although neither is an established improvement on this scene:

- **Progressive resolution.** The release exposes `progressive_resolution_start=4` and `progressive_resolution_full_at=0.3`. It can spend the first portion of training on fewer pixels, potentially reducing bilateral-grid, SSIM and raster work. A lower-resolution beginning may discard thin features or change densification and schedules, and changing the step count also changes the schedule. Compare a fresh run at the same total step count and another at equal wall time, same inputs/settings/seed, final full resolution and exact same raw holdouts. Measure pre-processing and transition costs, late-stage quality and fine detail; do not assume a 4× reduction in one dimension yields a 16× total speedup. Test mask downsampling and prevent invalid source pixels influencing valid loss. The default automatic schedule and validation are in the [pinned resolution scheduler](https://github.com/harry7557558/spirula-studio/blob/667e353bca3941303a4d93e93aa8ae3f2c202712/src/data/ResolutionSchedule.h).
- **Deficit view sampling.** The release exposes `view_sampling=deficit`. It changes which actual physical-lens camera is chosen, not the camera model. The [pinned data manager](https://github.com/harry7557558/spirula-studio/blob/667e353bca3941303a4d93e93aa8ae3f2c202712/src/data/DataManager.h#L118-L123) weights each view using the mean render count of splats it showed relative to the median, with a power and clamp. It balances under-rendered splats; it does not prioritize image error. Compare exposure counts per lens and timestamp, coverage and quality at equal time. It will not by itself skip pixel work on a selected view and can oversample bad pose or mask regions.
- **3DGUT compatibility requires a geometry gate.** [3DGUT](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Wu_3DGUT_Enabling_Distorted_Cameras_and_Secondary_Rays_in_Gaussian_Splatting_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf) replaces a local linearization with an unscented projection and evaluates a 3D Gaussian along pixel rays. It motivates a nonlinear projection comparison, not an assumed fidelity or speed win for this recording. In the pinned release, the [spherical unscented projection](https://github.com/harry7557558/spirula-studio/blob/667e353bca3941303a4d93e93aa8ae3f2c202712/src/shaders/primitive_3dgs.slang#L356-L386) unwraps sigma-point longitude and the source defines ERP forward and backward variants. The [Vulkan 3DGUT forward path](https://github.com/harry7557558/spirula-studio/blob/667e353bca3941303a4d93e93aa8ae3f2c202712/src/backend/vulkan/shaders/rasterize_fwd.slang#L348-L405) generates a camera-model-dependent ray per pixel; it uses projected AABBs for tile selection and culling. Thus projection support is present, but longitude-unwrapped covariance does **not** prove periodic tile coverage, correct 3D ray evaluation across the seam, poles, or backwards gradients. Audit the common AABB/tile path and run seam-shifted one-splat forward/backward and polar tests against an independent spherical ray reference on Vulkan before proposing a quality A/B. If these pass, compare 3dgs and 3dgut with the same two physical-lens images, rig poses, masks, split and raw holdouts; report geometry, kernel cost and end-to-end time. `mip` is another selectable primitive, not evidence of spherical anti-aliasing correctness; establish its ERP behavior and mask/downsample footprint first.

## Additional Google and Scaniverse evidence

[Google Research's DRGSplat (BMVC 2026)](https://research.google/pubs/drgsplat-depth-regularized-3d-gaussian-splatting/) constrains rendered depth through monocular depth, surface normals and uncertainty-aware flatness. Its abstract reports improved geometry while retaining photometric quality; it does not establish faster training or native ERP support. The page says code will be released, so it is not yet a verified implementation route. The useful direction here is geometry supervision with explicit uncertainty, after validating radial depth and spherical gradients. Treat estimated depth as a fallible prior, not observed pixels or metric truth.

[Scaniverse's own processing guide](https://dev.scaniverse.com/news/view-your-own-3d-photos-in-the-meta-quest-3-3s) documents 1–2 minute on-device processing after capture. That substantiates the product speed comparison, but does not disclose enough optimizer, camera-pose, resolution, representation or hardware details to attribute the result to one algorithm. Our comparison must include export, masking, poses and training; the guide is not an interchangeable benchmark for this two-lens recording.

## New measured constraints on the next experiments

- Raw render audits at the original training camera poses now pass exact retained-control tests: 32 unchanged reference/mask/render triplets for the first audit and 26 for the diverse audit. Four early training views measured 25.87–27.23 dB spherical PSNR; ten views across frames 885, 930, 1005, 1500 and 1995 span 14.34–26.62 dB. The unchanged checkpoint still loses fine detail at its training cameras. Doorway edges often coincide within a pixel while window/blind and fabric detail is lost. Nearest-edge distance can match the wrong edge and is not pose or geometry ground truth. See recording artifacts `rig-training-view-audit-20261115` and `rig-training-view-diverse-20261115`; the reusable audit is `tools/native-sfm/audit_training_views.py`.
- The 16-timestamp resolution diagnostic compares actual 1024 and 2048 source observations at fixed full-run poses, seed, masks and four heldout lens images. After area-downsampling for common comparison, mean PSNR changes 18.789 to 18.842 dB, SSIM .74467 to .75359. This short doorway experiment does not show a sharpness fix; it does not establish that higher resolution cannot help other views or a better-converged model.
- Exact-zero bilateral-gradient tile skipping passed 48,528 same-device output comparisons including production q16-grid/F16-RGB inputs, with max absolute difference 1.86e-9. Synthetic half-empty tiles cut this kernel's measured time by about 34–38%; this is not end-to-end training gain or measured real gradient sparsity. The first benchmark was invalid because its two binaries were identical; separate build directories and distinct hash checks now prevent that mistake. Layer 0004 remains disabled until integration is tested.
- The configured growth factor is 1.05 each 100 steps, subject to other population limits. The resolved `refine_stop_iter=14000` keeps refinement enabled through the old 10k run; the stop-last-2500 setting does not override that floor. A bounded experiment will test reaching the same 300k cap earlier and allocating fewer total steps to reach comparable quality. This is a schedule experiment motivated by early densification research, not an implementation of SAD-GS. Keep resolution, physical observations, pose set, masks, holdouts and appearance correction fixed; report per-view regressions and wall time before promotion.
