# Rig SfM quality and speed: research update

2026-11-15. This note supplements [the splat quality and speed update](splat-quality-speed-update.md) and [the native 360 tracking review](native-360-state-of-art.md). It addresses pose reconstruction for the two synchronized physical-lens ERP observations, not splat training. Reported paper results are their authors' results on their data, not predicted performance on this recording or Apple/AMD hardware. Preserve one body pose per timestamp, both fixed lens extrinsics, reviewed operator and support masks, and full spherical images. The actual effective lens baseline and metric scale remain unverified.

## Recommended first experiment: add observations around the weak interval

Use the existing native spherical global rig pipeline and SIFT before introducing a new matcher or mapper. On the reviewed 840–1065 interval, compare the existing 16 timestamps with a 31-timestamp set made by adding one synchronized source timestamp between each consecutive pair. Select the 15 additions from actual packet timestamps, not interpolated images. This local A/B tests whether more physical observations constrain the difficult doorway and motion transition; it is not evidence that doubling the entire clip will improve quality or speed.

- Keep the 16 shared image and mask files byte-identical, fixed rig configuration, binary, SIFT settings, exhaustive pairing, rotation-prior convention and strength, coordinate policy, and random seed where available. Review each new timestamp's two masks and record PTS and hashes. Apply the same per-lens held-out feature regions to every observation in both arms; retain the existing frozen tracks for comparison on the shared timestamps. The benchmark's tracks are not flawless: a previously identified false artwork track remains in the raw results. Report all tracks, and predeclare any separate verified-outlier sensitivity analysis.
- Record extraction, pair selection, matching, mapping, total elapsed time, output bytes, pair and inlier counts, connected components, registered bodies, fixed rig constraints, weak graph cuts, track length, angular parallax and reprojection distributions. Compute held-out angular residuals on the same original observations and report coverage and failures, per lens and around frames 930–960. Fused orientation entered the solve and is a consistency check, not independent ground truth. A single similarity alignment may compare shared poses between models, but a difference is not proof of which model is accurate.
- Gate on at least the original registration and connectivity, no material deterioration of common held-out residuals or the doorway, and an explicit per-stage runtime/storage budget. Inspect source-aligned image or geometry evidence; a lower training reprojection score alone is not acceptance. Review the 31-body result before extrapolating to the full recording. Additional adjacent frames can have small parallax and blur, amplify repeated-structure errors, and increase BA and matching work even when registered.

This is the least integration work because it uses the current executable, fixed-rig global solver and data preparation. The existing 138-body result registered 276/276 images in one model, while its training reprojection error and orientation priors are not independent accuracy. The earlier 16-body coordinate-flag test is a warning against trusting command success: with `SS_SFM_PIXEL_CENTER=1`, all 32 images appeared across two disconnected models (18 and 14), while OFF yielded one 32-image model. Only 15 of 92 frozen tracks remained comparable in the ON primary model. Keep the flag OFF for the next density A/B; this one case does not prove the correction is generally wrong. The 138-body sequential-plus-retrieval trial also ran slower than the earlier exhaustive trial under different execution conditions, so it is not an established speedup. See [rig comparison](rig-sfm-comparison.md); preserved paired artifacts are `rig-pixel-center-pair-20261115` and `rig-reviewed-sequential-20261115` beside the recording.

## Matching: spherical models are promising but not a drop-in speed fix

[EDM, CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Jung_EDM_Equirectangular_Projection-Oriented_Dense_Kernelized_Feature_Matching_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf), is a dense pair matcher designed for full ERP images. It uses spherical positional information and geodesic refinement. Its paper reports relative-pose gains on Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D under its own protocols; these do not establish pose quality or runtime for two partially supported physical-lens observations. The [authors' repository](https://github.com/jdk9405/EDM) offers inference and a pretrained checkpoint; it is not a ready-made fixed-rig multiframe SfM backend. The [authors' example](https://github.com/jdk9405/EDM/blob/main/test.py) selects CUDA when available and otherwise CPU; Apple MPS, AMD, mask handling, seam behavior on these partial-support images and full-rig integration are unverified. Do not download weights or port it until a small source-only correspondence study has an accepted evaluation and resource budget.

[SCCM, ACCV 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36545), is a more recent spherical coarse matcher with yaw-periodic position information, a tangent-plane metric and area-aware visibility on a RoMa scaffold. Its authors report a controlled improvement on their ERP protocol, including a comparison with EDM; this is promising correspondence research, not a demonstrated camera trajectory or matched-hardware speed result. The [official implementation](https://github.com/gandanlee/sccm) uses a CUDA example and learned weights; its documentation says non-Linux platforms use a PyTorch fallback and CPU inference is supported. MPS/Vulkan acceleration, performance here, our physical-support masks, exact rig consistency and the license/dependency fit remain unverified.

[LightGlue, ICCV 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Lindenberger_LightGlue_Local_Feature_Matching_at_Light_Speed_ICCV_2023_paper.html), adapts matching work to pair difficulty. The [authors' implementation](https://github.com/cvg/LightGlue) reports hardware-specific RTX 3080 throughput and warns that pruning overhead depends on hardware. Those comparisons are not against this project's Vulkan SIFT matcher or end-to-end SfM. The pinned Spirula source documents ALIKED + LightGlue and LoMa options, but the currently configured standalone build has `SS_BUILD_SAM=OFF` and `SS_NN_METAL=OFF`; learned inference and checkpoints are not enabled in that executable. Source availability is not evidence that the models run on this Mac or on AMD. A future matched frontend trial must first validate its inference backend and weights, feed the same full physical-lens ERP images and masks, keep pair candidates fixed, and compare verified correspondences and held-out rig geometry before measuring end-to-end time. Perspective-trained descriptors are not automatically spherical-aware; [SphereGlue's spherical matching study](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/IMW/papers/Gava_SphereGlue_Learning_Keypoint_Matching_on_High_Resolution_Spherical_Images_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf) illustrates this failure under yaw shifts.

[IM360, ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Jung_IM360_Large-scale_Indoor_Mapping_with_360_Cameras_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf), combines spherical dense matching, two-view geometry, incremental triangulation and spherical BA for pose estimation. Its later geometry stage uses cubemap projection; that later stage is outside this task's native tracking constraint. The [authors' repository](https://github.com/jdk9405/IM360) currently contains a placeholder rather than the implementation. Its evaluation assumes room membership or predefined overlap when choosing pairs, so pair discovery cost and robustness are not established by those experiments. It is evidence for evaluating a fully spherical matching-to-optimization chain, not an executable replacement or proof of fixed two-lens rig support.

## Global, incremental and rig constraints

[MGSfM, ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Tao_MGSfM_Multi-Camera_Geometry_Driven_Global_Structure-from-Motion_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf), explicitly models rigid multicamera units and combines relative-camera and feature-track constraints for global positioning. Its [authors' code](https://github.com/3dv-casia/MGSfM) accepts a COLMAP database and describes a single rig with overlapping or non-overlapping cameras. This is a useful reference for graph conditioning and initialization, but exact EQUIRECTANGULAR support, preservation of our specified nonzero fixed extrinsics, uncertainty of the baseline, GPU acceleration and Apple/AMD behavior are not established by that documentation. A port or comparison needs a compatible spherical database and a known-truth rig test first; it is not a low-cost toggle in the current runner.

[GLOMAP](https://lpanaf.github.io/eccv24_glomap/) motivates global initialization for scale. Its [official repository](https://github.com/colmap/glomap) reports migration into COLMAP's global mapper; its reported speed comparisons are dataset and implementation dependent. They do not prove this scene's global estimate is better than an incremental one. The pinned Spirula global mapper is itself experimental, and the project's earlier source audit found that its incremental mapper initially drops supplied nonzero rig translations. Therefore an incremental-versus-global comparison is not valid for this physical rig until a layerctl-maintained fix and a synthetic test verify that both paths preserve the same body poses and fixed extrinsics. Compare on identical verified graph and known truth before attributing any result to the solver. Do not replace the estimated rig baseline with a guessed metric value.

## Frame selection and runtime

A fixed sampling rate is a useful reproducible control, not a universal information optimum. [Submodular keyframe selection for SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05576) studies selecting observations for coverage and translation observability; its assumptions and SLAM setting do not establish the best subset of this rig sequence. After the local density A/B, consider a deterministic selection that keeps verified graph bridges, loop/revisit candidates, both synchronized lens images, adequate parallax and sharp support. Evaluate held-out timestamps and weak cuts; do not select based only on high match count or training reprojection, and do not discard the only bridge or choose frames after observing the evaluation scores.

Runtime must include source export, mask review/preparation, extraction, pair selection, matching, reconstruction, and downstream training/evaluation. Exhaustive candidate pairs scale from 496 at 32 images to 1,891 at 62 images, before image-level graph filtering. The pinned sequential option pairs within each lens folder and adds content retrieval; it does not guarantee all cross-lens edges, while retrieval itself scores candidate pairs. Measure it against exhaustive on the same data and retained graph, especially because the local 138-body sequential run did not beat the earlier exhaustive run. Do not treat reduced full-match count as a proven runtime win. Use matched GPU availability, cache/warm-up state, wall clocks and storage limits; separately verify AMD on hardware.
