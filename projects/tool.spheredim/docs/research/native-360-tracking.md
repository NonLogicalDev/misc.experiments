# Native 360 camera tracking: research notes

Checked 2026-11-14. Scope: pose estimation from the complete equirectangular frame, without extracting perspective or cubemap views. Reported paper results are authors' results, not independent validation of this port.

- [360DVO (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02309) is a learned monocular visual-odometry system. A distortion-aware feature network extracts sparse spherical patches; a recurrent network estimates correspondences and confidence, and spherical differentiable bundle adjustment refines camera poses and patch depths. Its authors report improvements over 360VO and OpenVSLAM on their chosen benchmarks. The released implementation uses PyTorch and custom NVIDIA CUDA operators. Our port replaces inference operators with PyTorch tensor equivalents, and its Apple results are measured separately in `validation/portable-360dvo`. Its output is monocular and scale-ambiguous. The paper's real-video reference trajectories are generated using SfM, so agreement with them is not an independent physical ground-truth measurement.
- [360VO (2022)](https://huajianup.github.io/research/360VO/360VO_ICRA2022.pdf) is direct sparse visual odometry using a spherical camera model on equirectangular images. It relies on brightness consistency and geometric optimization rather than the learned 360DVO correspondence network. Lighting changes, occlusion and moving objects are meaningful evaluation cases for a direct method.
- [stella_vslam](https://stella-cv.readthedocs.io/en/stable/overview.html) supports an equirectangular camera model and offers a feature-based SLAM comparison. SLAM can use mapping and loop closure; short-clip VO accuracy alone does not establish long-loop drift performance. Its camera model support does not by itself show that every image feature operation handles the seam or pole uniformly.
- [COLMAP](https://github.com/colmap/colmap/releases) has a native equirectangular camera model. The existing SphereDim path uses this for offline spherical SfM with complete panorama frames. It can globally optimize reconstructions but may lose frames or fail to initialize in difficult scenes. The earlier SphereDim tests record such failures; its SIFT feature extraction is not a learned distortion-aware panorama encoder.
- [PanoGS-SLAM (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17387) proposes spherical pose optimization and a sphere-aware photometric loss with a Gaussian map, initializing depth with a learned panorama depth estimator. The paper says code will be released. This is a promising research direction, not a verified drop-in monocular video solution here; the reported comparisons use its own datasets and protocol.
- [DROID-SLAM in the Wild (CVPR 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Li_DROID-SLAM_in_the_Wild_CVPR_2026_paper.html) studies uncertainty from inconsistent visual features in dynamic scenes. Its paper does not establish native spherical input compatibility. Its uncertainty treatment is a research idea for dynamic footage, not evidence that the unmodified system meets SphereDim's no-view-extraction requirement.

## Implications for SphereDim

Longitude is periodic; points near the left and right image boundaries are neighbors on the sphere. Equirectangular pixels also cover unequal spherical area, with distortion increasing near the poles. A fully spherical projection model alone does not ensure that patch sampling, learned feature convolutions, residuals and outlier checks all respect these properties. The upstream 360DVO residual and correlation behavior around the seam is inherited by the port and needs explicit experiments before changing it. Stitching artifacts, parallax between lenses, rolling shutter, blur, moving people, poor texture, pure rotation and exposure changes require separate tests.

The synthetic loop comparison uses a single similarity alignment to remove the unknown world frame and scale, then reports trajectory RMS normalized by path length and per-frame 95th percentile normalized image displacement. It does not establish metric scale or guarantee every pixel. Real-video metrics use the benchmark's reconstructed reference; use independent measured trajectories and held-out footage for a stronger claim. Full-sequence drift, repeated locations, difficult seams and cross-device parity remain open.
