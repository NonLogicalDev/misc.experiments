# SphereDim: native spherical tracking and reconstruction research

Research snapshot: 2026-11-15. Sources are primary papers, official code or official project documentation. Scope is a *single full equirectangular panorama per timestamp*, including spherical rays and local feature sampling; no splitting the panorama into perspective/cubemap images. A paper's reported results are its authors' results, not our measurements. Apple/AMD support is separate from paper accuracy. Private footage remains local.

## What the current evidence actually says

- **The present reconstruction is blurry and incomplete, and more splat iterations did not resolve it.** A 120-image, 1024×512, 500k-splat native ERP run finished 10k GPU steps. Provisional masked, unaligned 18-view scores at 1k/4k/6k/8k/10k were 19.885/20.660/20.699/20.792/20.612 dB PSNR and 0.7223/0.7346/0.7354/0.7343/0.7319 SSIM. Several held-out renders show strong blur/ghosting. This suggests a bottleneck other than merely iteration count, but it does not identify which one. The experimental mesh has holes and many disconnected components. See the evaluation notes and render comparison saved with the local recording.
- **The holdout evaluation is useful but not fully independent.** Tracker poses and sparse seed were derived from a recording including those frames. The full-checkpoint evaluator reports `train_frame_scale` 5.711 versus 5.667 in training, and held-out camera-specific appearance handling is unverified. Scene transforms read identity and the restored parameter arrays matched, but this does not resolve every equivalence concern. Do not compare the 20.6 dB result to unrelated papers or claim metric geometry accuracy from it.
- **Tracking geometry is fragile, but neither method has measured ground truth.** A GPU SIFT/native spherical SfM run using the same 120 masked images produced two disjoint models: 37 and 55 images, 92 distinct/120 total, no shared registered images. Their 0.546/0.444 px mean reprojection scores measure fitting of surviving observations, not trajectory truth; the remaining 28 were unregistered. Cross-method comparisons with the DVO trajectory are similarity-aligned agreement, not absolute error. Fixed-DVO spherical triangulation made a formally connected 120-camera graph, but removing tracks with <3 observations or <2° parallax split it into 113+7. This is evidence that some graph connectivity relies on weak tracks, not proof that every track is bad. Global real-data BA had non-positive-definite failures and did not converge. The existing user videos show substantial pitch/roll, which is a candidate stressor rather than an isolated proven cause.
- **The mask removes substantial observations.** Before 69 s, the conservative top-40% mask chosen for this recording removes about 34.5% of spherical solid angle. From 69 s onward top 75% leaves only ~14.6% of the sphere. The former 120-image experiment excluded the later part. The mask is justified by the observed operator, but its angular coverage and motion with camera roll mean it can remove useful scene evidence and make coverage uneven. This is a testable contribution, not proof of the main failure. Use explicit white-keep masks and exclude their pixels from feature extraction and losses; never train against a black fill as scene content.
- **The acquisition model is imperfect.** The X5 has two displaced lenses; a stitched sphere is not necessarily a single optical center, particularly near the seam and nearby objects. The imagery can contain blur, lens timing differences, stitching deformation and rolling-shutter effects. [Lo et al.](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/entities/publication/b4a1a072-0cd6-4a57-817a-b165669e6459) explicitly describe non-collocated lens centers, photometric mismatch and stitching distortion. These are plausible error sources here; their severity has not been measured for this clip. Do not infer that stabilization has corrected rolling shutter: our frame-level pose/mask mapping was verified only with rolling-shutter correction disabled.
- **The IMU helps rotation but is not a free trajectory.** The X5 recording provided 73,232 raw gyro/accelerometer samples, not absolute physical orientation. We fused an attitude estimate with arbitrary heading and possible drift; no absolute translation or scale is established. All timing, axis, shutter and stitch conventions must match the exact exported footage. Large accelerations can corrupt instantaneous gravity. The small real soft-position-prior BA sweep reduced a re-triangulated track diagnostic but repeatedly failed to converge and did not improve IMU relative-rotation agreement. A preliminary ERP/IMU synthetic solver also did not converge; neither is ready to apply.

A same-timestamp 120-frame native-SfM A/B later registered 109/120 distinct images in two horizon-level components versus 92/120 in two camera-relative components. Masks had an extra four-pixel exclusion guard in the stabilized run, and the stabilized images were resampled and re-encoded. This is evidence of better coverage in that experiment, not an isolated causal test or proof of accurate poses. The all-frame 2,162-image run was paused during feature extraction because the operator mask was too broad; its frame and feature caches were later cleared to recover disk space.

## Tracking and geometry: source landscape

1. [360DVO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02309) uses a distortion-aware spherical feature network and omnidirectional differentiable BA for monocular full-panorama odometry; its authors report benchmark improvements. SphereDim's MPS/ROCm-targeted port is a prototype and its results do not certify behavior on this clip, at extreme pitch/roll, near the ERP seam or under large masks. A VO system can also accumulate drift without explicit global closure. Treat its output as an initialization and measure forward/backward consistency, loop residuals and geometry support rather than calling its errors known.
2. [360VO](https://huajianup.github.io/research/360VO/360VO_ICRA2022.pdf) is a direct sparse method using a spherical camera and ERP images without rectification; it is a useful photometric/geometric baseline, not evidence that it will handle this clip or the required GPU backends. [COLMAP 4.2.1 spherical camera](https://github.com/colmap/colmap/blob/4.2.1/src/colmap/estimators/cost_functions/reprojection_error.h) supports equirectangular residuals with horizontal seam wrapping. SphereDim's actual 120-pose triangulation used this model and the real Spirula matches. Native reprojection/BA alone cannot resolve poor parallax or graph gauge. The installed PyCOLMAP Ceres tests were CPU diagnostics; do not imply an Apple/AMD GPU BA backend is validated.
3. [OpenVIO](https://github.com/93won/openvio) is MIT C++ visual-inertial odometry whose official [camera model](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/93won/openvio/master/doc/camera_models.md) supports a full ERP sphere, seam-wrapped residuals, a stitch-error parameter and two lens time offsets. Its [architecture](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/93won/openvio/master/doc/architecture.md) describes ordered IMU/image processing, optical-flow feature tracking, re-acquisition and an estimator; tangent-plane *local landmark patches* are used, rather than documented extraction of separate perspective camera images. A complete source audit is still needed to guarantee the whole selected path complies with the strict input constraint. Its authors claim online camera–IMU extrinsic/time and lens-time calibration; it has not been tested on this X5 or on our mask. Its [build configuration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/93won/openvio/master/CMakeLists.txt) is C++/OpenCV/Ceres and uses x86 flags in its fallback; Apple ARM and GPU execution are not established. It is a promising factor/calibration reference or CPU diagnostic, not a ready GPU solution.
4. [On-manifold IMU preintegration](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02363) is the standard principled model for summarizing high-rate inertial measurements between selected frames while handling rotation, covariance and bias. A practical spherical optimizer should couple true per-frame ERP bearings/landmarks with timestamped relative IMU rotation factors, estimated gyro bias and calibrated camera–IMU rotation/time offset, using robust uncertainty; use gravity only when justified by motion and acceleration. Absolute yaw is not observed by gravity, and a monocular trajectory does not acquire known metric scale merely by using attitude. Full visual-inertial metric estimation needs observability, correctly calibrated accelerometer/bias and motion, and validation.
5. The [360° optical-flow paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136950332.pdf) discusses the need for spherical angular metrics near poles. This supports testing error in angle as well as pixels. Some 360-flow and panorama methods create tangent/cubemap image views; exclude those variants under the user's constraint. ERP pixels sample unequal solid angle, so report per-latitude and solid-angle-weighted diagnostics, alongside the actual image-coordinate loss.

## Gaussian splats, depth and meshes

- [Spirula Studio](https://github.com/harry7557558/spirula-studio) is the practical current GPU baseline: official code says Vulkan/MoltenVK on Apple and AMD, native equirectangular models, white-keep masks, PLY seed and mesh export. Our Apple M2 Pro run verified 120 input → 120 processed cameras only with **both** `--warp-spherical-to-pinhole 0` and `--warp-to-pinhole 0`. The 360-camera preset otherwise defaults to six perspective faces and violates the scope. AMD operation remains untested. Its source is GPL-3.0; check terms when integrating.
- [PFGS360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23324) jointly reasons about pose, spherical rendering and consistent depth priors; [released code](https://github.com/zcq15/PFGS360) explicitly depends on CUDA, so it is a research reference or kernel-port candidate, not an Apple/AMD executable. Audit tangent feature operations and avoid perspective/cubemap outputs before reuse. Its reported benchmark performance is not measured here.
- [PanoGS-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17387) describes direct spherical photometric optimization, area-aware ERP loss and depth-guided initialization; the paper says source will be made public. It reports field-of-view conditioning trends, which make our heavy masks worth testing, not a proven causal explanation. [UniTriSplat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29794) describes equal-area spherical rasterization and a spherical-neighborhood SSIM; its quality claims and code/hardware applicability have not been independently established. These suggest controlled spherical-loss comparisons rather than declaring our rasterizer wrong.
- [ODGS-SLAM](https://github.com/odgs-slam/odgs-slam) documents native panorama RGB/RGB-D tracking and a spherical Gaussian rasterizer. It has native extension submodules and reported NVIDIA/CUDA requirements in its reproducibility setup; verify the exact dependency path before any MPS/ROCm port. Do not infer platform support from PyTorch alone.
- [DA360](https://github.com/antigravity-tech/DA360) is a panorama-specific monocular depth model with seam-conscious padding, available code and separately acquired weights. Its relative/scale-invariant output is a **prior**, not measured geometry; X5 mask, arbitrary poses and per-frame scale/temporal consistency must be tested. Audit model/weight license and Apple/AMD execution. Do not silently switch to perspective-crop depth methods.
- A splat is an appearance representation, not automatically a reliable surface. [2D Gaussian Splatting](https://github.com/IHKYoung/2DGS) and [SuGaR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12775) demonstrate surface-oriented parameterization/regularization and mesh extraction ideas. Their published implementations are not established here as native-ERP, Apple/AMD-compatible solutions. Holes and disconnected mesh pieces can arise from absent coverage, erroneous geometry, depth regularization and extraction settings; mesh-component count alone cannot identify a cause. Optimize and assess geometry explicitly, using measured multiview depth/normal consistency, coverage/uncertainty, and ground-truth scenes before cleanup or hole filling.

## Ranked practical tests

1. **Establish a trustworthy measurement loop before changing algorithms.** Record one manifest for all 2,162 source timestamps and per-frame mask, exposure/IMU mapping, blur score, unmasked solid angle, camera conventions and pose/track confidence. Preserve 0–69 s and 69–end as distinct regimes; the latter is heavily masked, not silently discarded. Use all eligible frames for tracking/temporal constraints or explicitly report why any are excluded; do not blindly train every near-duplicate frame. Prepare holdout intervals separated from training in time, and do not let their frames feed pose estimation, point seeding, depth supervision or hyperparameter selection when claiming independent image evaluation. To evaluate tracking independently, use controlled Blender spherical scenes with known 6-DoF paths/geometry, pitch/roll, varied texture, noise, blur, realistic masks and seam crossings; compare trajectory after one similarity transform and angular/image alignment. On real footage report self-consistency only unless an independent reference exists.
2. **Test rotation handling on exactly the same real frames.** A/B original camera-relative ERP and gyro-derived horizon-stabilized ERP, with the already verified per-frame OFF→FLOW_STATE direction transform and rolling-shutter correction off; map masks and convert pose axes consistently. Neither branch uses perspective images. Compare same frame-by-frame feature support, seam failures, pose registration, graph edges/cuts, IMU relative rotations, reprojection and track survival; avoid assuming stabilization must help. Because pixels are resampled, include an identity-resampling control. Separate fusion timing/bias/calibration and large-transient uncertainty from image optimization.
3. **Build a better spherical feature/pose graph before longer splat training.** Use all eligible timestamps for temporal linking, then choose keyframes based on coverage, parallax, sharpness and graph connectivity; retain revisits/loops. Maintain an actual time-varying keep mask with conservative dilation; if a less coarse operator segmentation is used, verify it removes the person on all frames and honors the user's intent. In particular, compare overlap and weak cuts around the 22–27 s separation. A position-only Sim(3) fit of disconnected components is a hypothesis, not a verified merge. Add robust bearing residuals, bias-aware relative gyro factors and pose priors with defensible covariances; inspect Jacobians, numerical rank, leverage and failure modes. The prior-based real prototype did not converge, and the synthetic relative-IMU CPU reference did not converge in its bounded tests. Do not promote either.
4. **Controlled splat experiments, not blind iteration.** Use *one* stable pose/mask/input dataset per comparison and train native ERP with no warping into pinhole. Compare fixed vs improved poses, sampling density, seed initialization and spherical-area weighting individually. Keep exposure and calibration settings fixed; disable learned depth/normal supervision unless its native ERP path is explicitly validated. Our published Spirula research notes warn that some versions' ERP unprojection for depth/normal supervision falls back to pinhole; audit the exact release before using those features: [upstream plan](https://github.com/harry7557558/spirula-studio/blob/master/docs/notes/sfm-in-process-plan.md). Use source frames with genuine temporal parallax and no masked pixels; avoid overcounting correlated frames. Compare held-out masked spherical renders, angular error, sharpness/ghosting, per-frame and per-latitude performance; 10k → 8k apparent regression is not proof of overfitting without independent evaluation.
5. **Treat dense point cloud and mesh as distinct outputs.** Once pose stability is demonstrated, triangulate spherical depth across frames and fuse only cross-view-consistent, visible points; report coverage/confidence and preserve holes where no geometry is observed. Separately evaluate mesh surface distance, normals, visibility, connected components and completeness on synthetic truth; texture/appearance and topology are not substitutes for geometric accuracy. Port the needed compute to wgpu/Vulkan/Metal kernels for Apple/AMD rather than assume NVIDIA CUDA research code will run; compare CPU and GPU numerics and test AMD on hardware.

## Cause and priority

We have strong evidence of missing/weak constraints and a splat quality plateau, and a reproducible numerical failure in an unconstrained real BA attempt. We do **not** know whether the dominant cause is pitch/roll, mask coverage, pose drift, weak depth/parallax, stitch geometry, blur, loss/rasterizer behavior, or a combination. The highest-value next result is a controlled full-frame spherical A/B with synchronized masks/IMU and a graph/pose-quality audit, followed by a convergent known-truth-validated optimization—not another unsupported single setting or a prettier mesh.
